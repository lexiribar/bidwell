Our Story

More than a century in Manchester

From sixteen people seeking a place of their own to a congregation whose ministries reshaped a neighborhood.

On Sunday, October 25, 1914, sixteen people from Allen Chapel A.M.E. were called to start a new place for worship in their community. This group wanted a place where people could have a voice and control over their religious affairs, and “receive all those who might desire the light of the gospel and thus receive for themselves the benefits of His rich promises.” That desire established the mission of the church that is today known as Bidwell Presbyterian Church.

During the early years, a small group of believers met to discuss the mission, vision, and structure of the church. Worship began in a building formerly owned by an A.M.E. congregation in Manchester on Chartiers Street (now Chateau Street). The first sermon was preached by Mrs. Rebecca Aldridge, evangelist for the A.M.E. Conference of the Pittsburgh District. One of the important guiding principles was that there was to be no bishop nor presiding elder, but rather self or congregational government.

A timeline

Pastors and turning points

  1. December 1914

    Rev. Mount Albert Dyer, first pastor

    The congregation called its first pastor. The early name of the church during that period was St. Luke Presbyterian Mission, a mission of Grace Memorial Presbyterian Church. The North Side mission continued to grow until the congregation successfully petitioned the Pittsburgh Presbytery for admission as an organized church. Rev. Dyer entered into eternal rest on April 9, 1916.

  2. 1916–1940

    Rev. Benjamin F. Glasco

    Installed as pastor on December 5, 1916. During his tenure, funds were raised, drawings completed, and property purchased for a new church building at the corner of Liverpool and Bidwell Streets. Construction was completed on January 19, 1919, and the church received the official name Bidwell Street Presbyterian Church. Rev. Glasco served until the spring of 1940, when he accepted a call from Berean Presbyterian Church in Philadelphia.

  3. 1940–1950

    Rev. Samuel G. Stevens

    Installed October 22, 1940. Rev. Stevens focused on building the youth and young adult ministries within the church and the community, adding to the growth of the congregation. Mrs. Gladys Stevens, responding to the needs of the church and community, organized a Sunday Nursery program. Rev. Stevens served until 1950, when he accepted a teaching position at Lincoln University.

  4. 1951–1960

    Rev. Virgil P. Moccia

    Bidwell installed the first white minister of an African American congregation in Pittsburgh. The church continued to thrive under Rev. Moccia’s leadership; a new chancel was built and the church kitchen expanded. He answered a call to pastor in Kansas City, Missouri in 1960, and was followed by Rev. William E. Johnson, who served as supply for two years and served as Minister of Youth for the Pittsburgh Presbytery.

  5. 1962–1997

    Rev. James J. Robinson

    Called from Brotherhood Presbyterian Church in Wichita, Kansas in August 1962. Soon after his installation, Bidwell celebrated its 50th Anniversary, October 4–9, 1964. Rev. Robinson served for more than thirty years, leading the church to focus on the most critical social issues of the time — housing, education, and the Black family. After the assassination of Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., Bidwell built a recreation center for youth and established a vocational training center to address unemployment in the community. Both continue today as the Bidwell Cultural and Training Center and the Manchester Youth Development Center. The congregation bestowed the title Pastor Emeritus in recognition of his 35 years of service.

  6. 1976–1977

    A new sanctuary

    On February 17, 1976, ground was broken at the corner of Bidwell and Liverpool Streets, opposite the old church building, for a new facility. It was dedicated on March 27, 1977.

  7. 1999–2006

    Rev. John C. Welch

    Came to Bidwell in September 1999 as a student minister, serving two years as Minister Intern and initiating the Bidwell Street Academy, a Saturday program for youth in the church and community. Together with his wife, Minister DeNeice Welch, he created a focus on praise and worship, and rejuvenated music and youth ministry through the Bidwell Youth Choir — work dating back to servants of the church such as Mr. Ralph Swan. Installed as pastor on December 28, 2003, he served until accepting the role of Vice President and Dean of Students at Pittsburgh Theological Seminary in 2006. Rev. Ronald R. Peters served as Community Minister and Interim Pastor during the period of reflection and transition.

  8. 2007

    Rev. Dr. B. DeNeice Welch

    On Sunday, April 1, 2007, the congregation extended a call to Rev. B. DeNeice Welch to serve as the fifth pastor of Bidwell Church, coming to the congregation after serving as Associate Pastor for Mission & Outreach at Shadyside Presbyterian Church.

  9. Today

    A season of transition

    Bidwell is currently seeking its next pastor. A Pastoral Nominating Committee, elected by the congregation, is carrying out the search. The ministries of this church — worship, study, music, youth, and outreach into Manchester — continue throughout.
