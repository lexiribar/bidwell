Our Story

More than a century in Manchester

From sixteen people seeking a church of their own to a congregation whose work reshaped a neighborhood.

On Sunday, October 25, 1914, sixteen people from Allen Chapel A.M.E. were called to start a new place for worship in their community. They wanted a church where people could have a voice and control over their own religious affairs, and where all who sought the light of the gospel would be received.

That desire became Bidwell Presbyterian Church. Worship began in a building on Chartiers Street, now Chateau Street, and the first sermon was preached by Mrs. Rebecca Aldridge, an evangelist of the A.M.E. Conference. From the beginning the congregation governed itself — no bishop, no presiding elder. Within two years it had grown enough to petition the Pittsburgh Presbytery for admission as an organized church.

In the century since, Bidwell has built two sanctuaries, weathered the upheavals that swept Pittsburgh’s North Side, and answered them by building things the neighborhood needed and did not have.

Moments that shaped us

  1. 1914

    A congregation begins

    Sixteen people gather to form what was first known as St. Luke Presbyterian Mission, a mission of Grace Memorial Presbyterian Church.

  2. 1919

    The first church building

    After years of fundraising, the congregation completes its own building at the corner of Liverpool and Bidwell Streets and takes the name Bidwell Street Presbyterian Church.

  3. 1964

    Fifty years

    The congregation marks its golden anniversary with a week of celebration in October.

  4. Late 1960s

    Answering a neighborhood in crisis

    In the era of the civil rights movement and the assassination of Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., Bidwell turned to the most pressing issues facing Manchester: housing, education, and the Black family. The church built a recreation center for young people and founded a vocational training center to address unemployment. Both endure today as the Manchester Youth Development Center and the Bidwell Cultural and Training Center.

  5. 1977

    The sanctuary we worship in today

    Ground was broken in February 1976 across from the old church, and the new building was dedicated on March 27, 1977 — the round brick sanctuary that still stands at Bidwell and Liverpool.

  6. Today

    A season of transition

    Bidwell is seeking its next pastor, with an interim minister leading the congregation and a Pastoral Nominating Committee carrying out the search. Worship, study, music, youth ministry, and outreach into Manchester continue throughout.
