Leadership

Those who have served this pulpit

For more than a century, Bidwell has been led by pastors who understood the church as something that happens in the neighborhood as much as in the sanctuary. We are now prayerfully seeking the next of them.

Since 1914

A history of leadership

Nine ministers have served Bidwell since sixteen people gathered to form this congregation in 1914 — through two church buildings, the civil rights era, and the long work of rebuilding Manchester.

Select a name to read more.

  • Rev. Mount Albert Dyer 1914 – 1916

    Called in December 1914 as the congregation’s first pastor, when the church was known as St. Luke Presbyterian Mission. He led the young congregation until his death on April 9, 1916.

  • Rev. Benjamin F. Glasco 1916 – 1940

    Installed December 5, 1916. During his twenty-four years, the congregation raised funds, purchased the property at Liverpool and Bidwell Streets, and completed its first church building in January 1919 — taking the name Bidwell Street Presbyterian Church. He left in 1940 for Berean Presbyterian Church in Philadelphia.

  • Rev. Samuel G. Stevens 1940 – 1950

    Installed October 22, 1940. He built up the youth and young adult ministries of the church and neighborhood, growing the congregation. Mrs. Gladys Stevens organized a Sunday Nursery program in response to the needs of church and community. He left in 1950 to teach at Lincoln University.

  • Rev. Virgil P. Moccia 1951 – 1960

    In calling Rev. Moccia, Bidwell installed the first white minister of an African American congregation in Pittsburgh. The church thrived under his leadership; a new chancel was built and the kitchen expanded. He answered a call to Kansas City, Missouri in 1960.

  • Rev. William E. Johnson 1960 – 1962

    Served as supply pastor for two years, continuing Bidwell’s focus on young people. He also served as Minister of Youth for the Pittsburgh Presbytery.

  • Rev. James J. Robinson — Pastor Emeritus 1962 – 1997

    Called from Brotherhood Presbyterian Church in Wichita, Kansas in August 1962, he served more than thirty years — the longest pastorate in Bidwell’s history. Under his leadership the church took up the most critical social issues of the era: housing, education, and the Black family. After the assassination of Rev. Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr., Bidwell built a recreation center for youth and founded a vocational training center to address unemployment in the neighborhood. Both endure today as the Manchester Youth Development Center and the Bidwell Cultural and Training Center. The congregation named him Pastor Emeritus in recognition of 35 years of service.

  • Rev. Dr. Ronald R. Peters Late 1990s — Interim Pastor

    Served as Community Minister and Interim Pastor during a period of reflection and transition for the congregation.

  • Rev. John C. Welch 1999 – 2006

    Arrived in September 1999 as a student minister and served two years as Minister Intern, founding the Bidwell Street Academy, a Saturday program for neighborhood youth. With his wife, Minister DeNeice Welch, he renewed the church’s focus on praise and worship and revived its music and youth ministry through the Bidwell Youth Choir — work rooted in the earlier service of members such as Mr. Ralph Swan. Installed as pastor December 28, 2003, he served until becoming Vice President and Dean of Students at Pittsburgh Theological Seminary in 2006.

  • Rev. Dr. B. DeNeice Welch Called in 2007

    Called by the congregation on April 1, 2007, after serving as Associate Pastor for Mission & Outreach at Shadyside Presbyterian Church. Born in West Virginia and raised in New Jersey, she holds a BA from Robert Morris College, a BS in Community Ministry from Geneva College, and a Master of Divinity from Pittsburgh Theological Seminary. At Shadyside she coordinated mission trips to the Gulf Coast after Hurricane Katrina and to Presbyterian partners in Malawi. As Bidwell’s Evangelism Ministry Chair she launched the summer lunch and camp program for children in Manchester, and she brought to the pulpit a passion for prayer, praise, mission, and evangelism.

    “Mission is an area where we, as servants of God, are afforded the opportunity to demonstrate God’s love for people who have difficulty seeing that love in their lives. In God’s economy, those who give of themselves receive much more than they can imagine.” Rev. Dr. B. DeNeice Welch
  • Rev. Dr. Ronald Peters Today — Interim Minister

    Rev. Dr. Peters leads this congregation through its present season of transition, as he did once before, while the Pastoral Nominating Committee seeks Bidwell’s next installed pastor.

Where we are now

Seeking our next pastor

Bidwell Presbyterian Church is in a season of pastoral transition. Our Pastoral Nominating Committee has been elected by the congregation and is actively at work seeking the minister God is calling to serve this church next.

Rev. Dr. Ronald Peters is serving as our interim minister through this season — a role he also filled at Bidwell during an earlier time of transition. Worship, study, and the ministries of this congregation continue under his leadership while the search goes on.

A pastoral search in the Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.) is a deliberate, prayerful process, and it belongs to the whole congregation rather than to any one person. The Session and congregation first take stock of who we are and where God is leading us. The congregation then elects a Pastoral Nominating Committee — the PNC — to carry out the search on its behalf.

The PNC prepares a Ministry Information Form describing Bidwell, our neighborhood, and the gifts we are seeking in a pastor. That form is shared through the denomination so that ministers across the country can learn about us. The committee then reviews the candidates who respond, listens to their preaching, interviews them, and prays over the discernment together.

When the committee agrees on a candidate, that name goes to the Pittsburgh Presbytery’s Committee on Ministry, and then to the congregation. The whole congregation votes on whether to extend a call. Only then is a new pastor installed.

How you can take part

Pray. The committee has asked for the congregation’s prayers throughout the search — for clear discernment, for patience, and for the minister who does not yet know Bidwell is waiting for them.

Keep showing up. Worship, Bible study, and the ministries of this church continue exactly as they have. A congregation in transition is still a congregation at work.

Talk with the committee. Members of the PNC welcome the thoughts of the congregation about what Bidwell needs in its next pastor.

For ministers considering Bidwell

If you have found this page while discerning a call, you are looking at a congregation founded in 1914 by people who wanted a church of their own voice, which built a recreation center and a job training center when its neighborhood needed them, and which is still rooted in Manchester on Pittsburgh’s North Side.

We would be glad to hear from you.
